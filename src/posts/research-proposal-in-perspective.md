As a master’s student, creating a research proposal can feel like a Herculean task. While I had worked on my mini dissertation during my undergraduate degree, I did not have a full understanding of research methods and concepts that i used. My undergraduate work, which I am very proud of, exposed me to the process of writing scientific papers. It was co-authored with my classmates and dear friends, teaching me the beauty and strength of collaboration. Successful it may have been, but I was out of my depth trying to understand p-values, hazard/risk ratios, sampling techniques etcetera.  

As a first-time master’s student, my role is to integrate the deep learning and understanding I have gained in the subjects of epidemiology and (bio)statistics with the process of research I was exposed to as an undergraduate student. It is me thinking as originally as possible, weighing my knowledge, skill-set and competency, and the time constraints i have against the very big dreams of being an influential and impactful researcher and academician someday. Stepwise and maybe the lengthy process of having research output can be daunting (or at least it is in my experience).
